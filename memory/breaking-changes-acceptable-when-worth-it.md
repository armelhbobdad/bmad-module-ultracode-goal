# Breaking changes acceptable when worth it

The user is open to breaking changes when the benefit justifies them: after the zero-regression sweep reported its needs-decision items, "I could be open to breaking changes if it worth it." (2026-08-04, two days after v2.0.0, when the install base was one project, GMC). That authorised a "worth-a-major-sweep" of breaking candidates (K>1 story driving against `drive_epic.py`'s one-story-per-process premise, `--parallel` retirement, run-status.json/health-check contract modernisation) with the burden of proof on the break and a non-breaking alternative required for each proposal; that sweep concluded nothing earned a major at the time. So a future session should not treat the docs/_internal/STABILITY.md contract as frozen: present breaking proposals with quantified benefit, migration cost and a non-breaking alternative and let the user rule; the stance is conditional, not a standing approval of any specific break. One candidate was parked unjudged with a stated reopen condition rather than rejected: K>1 story driving (`--stories-per-spawn`) against the premise that `STORIES_PER_SESSION = 1` is a constant in `drive_epic.py` and [getting-started](../docs/getting-started.md) states "One story per process", where the sweep estimated that the `preflight_check.py --assert-armed` resume manifest (PR #90) captures most of K=2's saved turns and wrote the operator question it deliberately did not ask ("May per-story context death be demoted from invariant to default (K=1, cap 4)...?") with the condition "Do not ask it until Tier 1.2 has shipped and the residual resume tax is re-measured in production". The user's "go on." approved the roadmap as a whole, not that question, so it stays open at v2.2.0 and closes on a user ruling, a `--stories-per-spawn` flag, or production re-measurement, never on another estimate.
